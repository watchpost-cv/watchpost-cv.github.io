Collection

Collection is where operational truth enters Watchpost. The design gives equal weight to provenance and failure behaviour—not just the happy-path numeric value.

First-party coverage

The initial programme covers host CPU, memory, disks, load, uptime, and processes, followed by HTTP, TCP, DNS, and TLS checks. Read-only SNMPv3 then adds network-device posts, followed by UPS/PDU and environmental-sensor posts.

Cameras, arbitrary consumer IoT, PLC writes, and building-control commands remain future exploration areas rather than permanent exclusions. Cameras should begin with availability and health; IoT needs bounded adapters; PLC and building-control work requires a separate threat model and must never be represented as safety control.

Observation envelope

Every accepted observation is attributable and bounded. The proposed envelope includes:

  • stable post and collector identity;
  • observation time and ingestion time;
  • signal name, type, value, and unit;
  • quality and freshness state;
  • bounded labels and optional structured evidence;
  • collector contract version and sequence/replay information.

Push and pull

Pull checks suit reachable posts; authenticated push supports isolated or ephemeral environments. Neither escapes payload limits, identity, rate control, replay defence, or backpressure.

Cardinality and volume

Labels, logs, process lists, and per-request dimensions can exhaust memory and disk before raw sample count does. Watchpost exposes budgets and rejected or dropped data instead of hiding them.

Important invariant

A collector failure must be visible as collector failure. It must not manufacture healthy post data or silently preserve an old value as current.

Collector isolation

Third-party integrations use an explicit protocol or process boundary rather than arbitrary plugins inside the primary server. Exact extension mechanics remain to be designed.
